UX case study · v1

See the pressure before it becomes a crash.

beforeburn combines a calendar-aware workload view with short immersive resets.

Mobile firstStudents firstPlanning + recoveryConcept stage

Problem

Calendars show commitments. They do not show whether a week is sustainable.

Promise

Spot overloaded periods and add recovery before exhaustion builds.

Primary user

High-school and college students balancing study, work, sport, and family.

Core action

Add a suggested reset or nap directly between calendar events.

Research questions

  • What makes a warning feel accurate rather than intrusive?
  • Will users connect a calendar during onboarding?
  • Which inputs are acceptable: time, sleep window, energy, demand?
  • Will users add a suggested break to their real calendar?
  • Do three immersive environments plus one nap provide enough choice?

Principles

One decision per screen

No dashboards of secondary metrics.

Explain every warning

State the schedule pattern causing it.

Suggest, never control

No calendar change without confirmation.

Rest without performance

No reset score, streak, or guilt.

Success measures

≥70%

Complete onboarding and view an imported week.

≥40%

Act on one break suggestion in week one.

≤15 sec

Median time to start a Reset.

Targets are initial hypotheses for prototype validation.

UI rules

Spacing

4px base. 24–32px between sections.

Shape

8–16px radius. No decorative containers.

Colour

Blue signals action. Load also uses labels and patterns.

Motion

150–240ms UI transitions. Recovery visuals: slow and optional.

Imagery

Only inside immersive Recovery experiences.

Voice

Neutral, specific, non-judgmental.

Content examples

UseAvoid

“Add a 15-minute reset?”“You need to calm down.”
“Not now”“Ignore recommendation”
Accessibility, safety, privacy

Requirements

Contrast

WCAG 2.2 AA. Never rely on colour alone.

Targets

Minimum 24×24 CSS px; prefer 44×44 on mobile.

Motion

Respect Reduce Motion. Provide static Reset visuals.

Audio

Volume, pause, captions/transcript for spoken guidance.

Calendar

Read-only by default. Ask before writing an event.

Health language

Planning support only. No diagnosis or prevention claims.

WHO defines burnout specifically as an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition. Student-facing language should use “load,” “energy,” and “recovery,” not diagnostic labels.

Participants

5–7 students aged 16–22 with mixed schedules. Include at least two who use separate school and personal calendars.

Decision gates

  • If calendar permission is unclear, rewrite onboarding before visual polish.
  • If warnings feel inaccurate, simplify the model and show the reason.
  • If four recovery choices slow selection, recommend one by default.
  • If users expect medical advice, revise positioning and language.

  5. NASA/FAA nap research: a 40-minute planned rest opportunity produced about 26 minutes of sleep and improved later alertness/performance in long-haul pilots.
  6. WHO — Burn-out as an occupational phenomenon: terminology and claim boundary.
  7. W3C — WCAG 2.2: accessibility requirements.
  8. Google Calendar — Permissions: granular calendar access and control.
